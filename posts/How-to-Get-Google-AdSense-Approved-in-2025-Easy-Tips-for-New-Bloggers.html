How to Get Google AdSense Approved in 2025: Easy Tips for New Bloggers

By Ubaid Ali — July 07, 2025
Tags: Google AdSense, Blogging, Make Money Online

Struggling to get your blog approved for Google AdSense? Many bloggers receive rejection emails, but the fixes are often straightforward. This guide provides simple, honest tips to improve your chances, illustrated with real stories from publicly shared experiences. It’s written in plain English, original, and aimed at helping new bloggers take practical steps toward approval.

Title: How to Get Google AdSense Approved in 2025: Easy Tips
Description: Get Google AdSense approved in 2025 with simple steps. Learn from real bloggers’ stories, improve your content, and grow real traffic—no shortcuts, just honest advice.
Keywords: AdSense approval 2025, blogging for beginners, earn money blogging, Google AdSense tips

This post is unique and created from scratch. Examples are summarized from public sources (blogs and forums) to ensure originality and compliance with Google’s rules.

1. Pick a Good Domain and Hosting

Google tends to trust websites that look professional. Buy your own domain (from providers such as GoDaddy or Namecheap) and choose reliable hosting (for example, Bluehost or SiteGround). New domains often need several months (commonly 3–6 months) to build trust; applying too soon increases the chance of rejection.

Blogger with a custom domain can work, but free WordPress.com plans do not support AdSense. Self-hosted WordPress (WordPress.org) with your own domain gives you more control and usually better chances for approval.

Real story: On a public forum, one blogger described getting AdSense approvals multiple times. They waited a month after buying the domain, used fast hosting, and installed caching tools like WP Rocket. After adding a contact page and improving speed, one site was approved within two days.

2. Create Helpful Content

Google favors original, useful posts that solve problems. Don’t copy or lightly rephrase other articles—write genuinely helpful content. Aim for 20–30 posts with 800–1,000 words each, use images for clarity, and break content into readable sections for mobile users.

Add essential site pages: About, Contact, Privacy Policy, Terms of Use, and a Disclaimer. Missing these pages is a common reason for rejection.

Real story: A nutrition blog that started in 2012 focused on original, high-quality content and grew to 15,000 daily visits. Over time they earned substantial AdSense revenue by prioritizing user experience and avoiding aggressive ad placements.

3. Bring in Real Visitors

Google does not publish a required traffic threshold, but real visitors are necessary to earn and sustain ads. Promote your content on platforms like X, Pinterest, and relevant forums. Never use fake traffic services or bots—these violate AdSense policies and can lead to account suspension.

Make sure your site loads quickly and is mobile-friendly. Use PageSpeed Insights to check speed and follow recommendations.

Real story: Mónika (a publicly described blogger) was rejected three times for thin content and poor mobile UX. After improving posts, optimizing for mobile, and focusing on organic search traffic, she received approval and continued to grow.

4. Stay Patient with Rejections

Rejections are common and often fixable. Read Google’s feedback carefully, address the issues (content quality, missing pages, slow speed), and reapply after making improvements. It may take weeks or months, so persistence matters.

Paid or free hosting options can work, but investing in a custom domain and reliable hosting increases professionalism and options for customization.

Real story: The forum blogger mentioned earlier faced three rejections for missing policy pages and speed issues. After adding the required pages and using a professional contact email, their site was approved within 28 days.

5. Think Long-Term

Running a successful blog involves ongoing work and costs—hosting typically runs $5–$10/month for basic plans. Earnings vary widely by niche, country, and traffic quality; ad revenue can range from cents to several dollars per thousand impressions depending on niche. Many publishers experience slow growth at first.

Avoid courses promising instant income—many push risky practices that can get you penalized by Google. Focus on organic growth, quality content, and honest promotion strategies.

Real story: A long-running blog took a year to reach consistent monthly revenue; by testing ad placements and improving SEO, the site eventually scaled to significant long-term earnings.

Key Points

  • Buy a custom domain and use reliable hosting to appear professional.
  • Publish 20+ original, useful posts and include policy pages.
  • Drive real visitors via search and social media—never use bots.
  • Fix issues after rejections and reapply with patience.
  • Plan for hosting costs and focus on long-term, honest growth.
